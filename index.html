The problem

Every service should not have to solve identity on its own

People use many different services, but those services often need to establish the same basic things.

Who is this person?
Are they old enough?
Are they allowed to access this service?
Are they where they are supposed to be?
Is this really the person performing this action?

Sojaly provides the identity and verification layer behind those interactions, so organisations and applications can rely on verified identity without building the entire system themselves.

Sojaly ID

One verified identity that can be used across services

Sojaly ID gives a person a verified digital identity that can be used when a service needs to establish who they are or verify something about them.

A person keeps their identity with Sojaly. Organisations and applications can request the verification they need without having to collect and manage the same identity information themselves.

Verification

Ask the question you actually need answered

A service does not always need someone's complete identity information.

Sometimes it only needs to know whether someone is over 18. Sometimes it needs to know whether they are at an approved location. Sometimes it needs confirmation that they are really the person authorised to perform an action.

Sojaly lets applications ask for those specific answers.

The service receives the verification result it needs. The underlying identity information does not have to cross with it.

Privacy

The answer crosses. The underlying data does not.

When an application asks whether someone meets a requirement, it does not necessarily need all the information used to answer that question.

If an application needs to know whether someone is over 18, it can receive that answer without receiving their date of birth.

If it needs to know whether someone is at an approved place, it can receive the verification result without receiving their location history.

Sojaly is designed around this distinction: give the service the information it needs for the decision, while protecting the underlying identity information.
